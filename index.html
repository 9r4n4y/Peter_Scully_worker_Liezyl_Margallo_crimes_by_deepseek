PART I

🕯️ Origins & Early Life

1.1 — Birth and Geographic Origin

Liezyl Margallo is a Filipina native. Multiple sources identify her as hailing from Misamis Oriental, specifically the municipality of Claveria, Cagayan de Oro in Northern Mindanao, Philippines. ↗1 Another report states she was from San Miguel, Manolo Fortich in Bukidnon, also within the Northern Mindanao region. This apparent discrepancy may reflect different family registrations or multiple residences during her early years. ↗2

1.2 — Childhood Abuse

Margallo herself claimed she was physically abused by her own mother as a child — she said she was "maltreated by her mother." ↗3 She told investigators that she wanted her victims to go through the same ordeal she had suffered, and that she "found pleasure in doing it." ↗4

"Apparently, that was a traumatic experience for her… She wanted to retaliate by hurting children, and she found pleasure in doing it."
— Atty. Janet Francisco, Head of the NBI Anti‑Human Trafficking Division ↗5

Psychologist Maryjun Delgado opined that Margallo's behavior was "clearly perverted" and that she "wants to hurt kids to appease her experiences. She wants to feel how it is to be an abuser, and she enjoys what she's doing. And yes, she is not afraid to kill." ↗6

1.3 — Victim of Trafficking

Multiple reports confirm that Margallo was herself a victim of child trafficking before she became a perpetrator. ↗7 One detailed analysis states: "then‑19‑year‑old Liezyl Margallo, who had previously been trafficked as a child, to commit some of the most severe physical assaults on the children." ↗8

1.4 — Entry into Prostitution and Meeting Peter Scully

By her teenage years, Margallo was working as a prostitute in the Philippines. ↗9 Reports indicate she met Australian national Peter Gerard Scully when she was just 14 years old. ↗10 Scully had fled Australia in 2011 to escape fraud charges and was establishing a trafficking network in the Philippines. Margallo became his live‑in partner and soon began participating actively in his criminal enterprise. ↗11

PART II

💀 The Criminal Enterprise (2011–2015)

2.1 — The "No Limits Fun" (NLF) Dark‑Web Operation

Scully and Margallo jointly ran a dark‑web child sexual abuse website called "No Limits Fun" (NLF) that offered pay‑per‑view video broadcasts of children being sexually assaulted and tortured. ↗12 The operation was described as a "lucrative international child sexual abuse ring." ↗13 Members and clients came from Brazil, Germany, the United States, and the United Kingdom. ↗14

2.2 — Modus Operandi — Luring, Drugging, and Filming

Margallo, Scully, and later accomplices including Carme Ann Alvarez and Maria Dorothea Chi y Chia would entice impoverished street children and scavengers with promises of food, schooling, and a comfortable life. ↗15 Once under their control, the children were drugged — sometimes on the advice of a Brazilian physician, Dr. Haniel Caetano de Oliveira, who instructed the ring on methods to dope victims so they would not resist during rape. ↗16 The children were then forced to perform sexual acts while being videotaped for a global audience. ↗17 Customers paid up to US $10,000 per view to livestream the abuse. ↗18

2.3 — "Daisy's Destruction" — The Infamous Video

The most notorious video produced by the ring was "Daisy's Destruction." ↗19 It is a three‑part video that features Margallo as the masked, naked woman who tortures an 18‑month‑old baby girl while Scully operates the camera. ↗20 The documented acts in the video included:

  • Hanging the baby upside down by her feet, hands tied and legs drawn apart ↗21
  • Whipping the child repeatedly with a belt ↗22
  • Taping the baby's mouth with duct tape to silence her cries ↗23
  • Dripping melted candle wax on the child's genitals ↗24
  • Clipping the baby's breasts with clothespins ↗25
  • Urinating on the abused child ↗26

Dutch police provided the video to Philippine authorities. NBI‑7 Assistant Director Dominador Cimafranca described it as "the worst video we have seen in our years of campaigning against child pornography." ↗27

2.4 — The Victims

The couple victimized at least 9 to 11 children, all girls, aged 1 to 12 years. ↗28 In addition to "Daisy" (the 18‑month‑old), the known victims included:

  • "Cindy" — An 11‑ or 12‑year‑old girl who died from repeated sexual abuse, strangulation, and internal hemorrhage. ↗29
  • "Queenie" and "Daisy" (the older) — Two cousins, aged 9 and 12, who were forced to dig their own graves after a failed escape attempt. ↗30
  • "Barbie" — A young child allegedly kidnapped from her family by Margallo. ↗31

2.5 — The Murder of "Cindy" (the 12‑Year‑Old)

According to Margallo's own account, Scully raped and tortured Cindy, then forced her to dig her own grave before strangling her with a rope. ↗32 Her body was buried under the floorboards of a rented apartment in Surigao City. ↗33 Margallo later pointed investigators to the location of the remains in 2015. ↗34 The cause of death was listed as strangulation and internal hemorrhage. Reports also mention a bolo (machete) being inserted in the child's butt and that kitchen knives were inserted inside her private parts, causing fatal bleeding. ↗35

2.6 — The 2015 Raid and Scully's Arrest

Scully was arrested on February 20, 2015 in Malaybalay, Bukidnon by NBI agents with assistance from the Australian Federal Police. ↗36 Margallo was initially allowed to go free because authorities lacked sufficient evidence against her at the time. ↗37 (Note: Some reports incorrectly state that Margallo "cooperated in the arrest of Scully in 2015," but detailed investigative journalism confirms she was merely released due to lack of warrants.) ↗38

PART III

👤 Life on the Run (2015–2017)

3.1 — Fugitive Status and Outstanding Warrants

After Scully's arrest, Margallo became a fugitive with 16 outstanding arrest warrants issued by courts in Cagayan de Oro City. ↗39 The charges included: qualified trafficking, child abuse, indecent shows, obscene exhibitions and publications, syndicated child pornography, photo and video voyeurism, and rape by sexual assault. ↗40

3.2 — Luxurious Lifestyle While Wanted

Despite the nationwide manhunt, Margallo lived a life of luxury. ↗41 She rented a condominium unit in uptown Cebu City for P13,000 a month (~US $260 at the time). ↗42 She was a regular member of a posh fitness center, traveled to tourist destinations like Bohol, and worked as a tour guide who also offered sex for a fee. ↗43

3.3 — Identity Theft: "Shannon Carpio"

Margallo assumed the alias "Shannon Carpio," reportedly posing as the wife of a French software millionaire. ↗44 She also held a passport under the name "Gina Carpio Batulan." ↗45 Her Facebook profile (under Shannon Carpio) showed her in glamorous photographs: revealing clothes, beach bikini shots (one topless from behind), and gym selfies. ↗46

She would shout her friends to drinks and dinners, claiming the money came from her "rich husband" and "people who loved her." ↗47 Friends described her as "a nice girl with an outgoing, sweet and innocent personality." ↗48 She even hosted a Sinulog party two weeks before her arrest and planned international trips to at least two Asian cities. ↗49

3.4 — Continuing the Trade from Prison

Investigators believe that Scully continued to run the cyber‑pornography business from prison, and that Margallo continued recruiting potential victims for new abuse videos. ↗50 One friend, Juliet Mejares, told Cebu Daily News that Margallo maintained regular communication with Scully while he was in detention. ↗51

PART IV

⚖️ Arrest & Court Proceedings (2017)

4.1 — The Arrest on Malapascua Island

On Wednesday, January 25, 2017 (around noon), NBI agents (NBI‑7), with the help of the Australian Federal Police, located and arrested Margallo while she was walking on the beach of Malapascua Island, a tourist spot off the town of Daanbantayan, Cebu. ↗52 She was in the company of two British nationals who were briefly questioned but later released after presenting valid travel documents. ↗53

When agents first approached her, she claimed she was someone else, but admitted her identity after being shown copies of the arrest warrants and her photograph. ↗54

4.2 — Immediate Court Appearance

Margallo was flown from Cebu to Cagayan de Oro City on Friday, January 27, 2017, and brought before Judge Jose Escobido of the 37th Branch of the Regional Trial Court. ↗55 She was also presented before Judges Evelyn Nery and Richard Mordeno of RTC Branches 19 and 22, who had issued the 16 arrest warrants. ↗56 She was then remanded to the city jail in Lumbia, Cagayan de Oro. ↗57

At the time, Margallo faced 59 cases (later expanded to 60). The charges included violations of:

  • RA 9995 — Anti‑Photo and Video Voyeurism Act of 2009 ↗58
  • RA 9610 — Special Protection of Children Against Child Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act ↗59
  • RA 9208 — Anti‑Trafficking in Persons Act of 2003 ↗60
  • RA 10175 — Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012 ↗61

4.3 — Public Outrage and Death Penalty Demands

The news of Margallo's arrest triggered an explosion of public outrage. ↗62 The Cebu Daily News headline "Savage Girl Falls" generated 504,000 shares in a single day. ↗63 Netizens called for the revival of the death penalty, describing her as a "sick, demented, poor excuse of a human being" and "worse than an animal." ↗64 Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte was already pushing to reintroduce capital punishment, and this case became a rallying point. ↗65

4.4 — Defense Claims of Remorse

In interviews with NBI lawyer Janet Francisco, Margallo expressed remorse: "I sat down with her and she told me she regretted everything she did to the kids." ↗66 Francisco noted that Margallo "has to answer for everything she committed against the children. Inasmuch as we pity her, justice must be served." ↗67

Margallo also claimed she had been planning to move out of the Philippines to start a new life abroad before she was caught. ↗68

4.5 — Legal Posture of Peter Scully

While Margallo's case progressed, Scully (already in detention) had pleaded not guilty to 75 charges and was forcing at least 10 of his alleged victims to testify against him. ↗69 Margallo's arrest was seen as a major blow to his defense, as she was expected to testify against him in court. ↗70

PART V

🔒 Conviction & Sentencing (2018–2022)

5.1 — First Conviction (2018) — Life Imprisonment for Scully and Alvarez

In June 2018, a Cagayan de Oro court convicted Scully and his other live‑in partner, Carme Ann Alvarez, of qualified trafficking and five counts of rape by sexual assault, sentencing them to life imprisonment and fines of P5 million plus moral and exemplary damages. ↗71 Alvarez had been Scully's girlfriend since she was a 14‑year‑old prostitute and had also offered food and shelter to lure victims. ↗72 Margallo was still facing her own separate batch of cases at this point. ↗73

5.2 — Final Conviction (2022) — 126 Years for Margallo

On September 3, 2022 (some reports cite November 3, 2022; this may reflect different promulgation dates for the same batch of cases), a court in Cagayan de Oro sentenced Margallo to 126 years in prison. ↗74 Scully received a 129‑year sentence at the same proceeding. ↗75

The couple faced 60 charges in that second batch, including:

  • Trafficking in persons ↗76
  • Child pornography ↗77
  • Child abuse ↗78
  • Rape of children as young as 18 months ↗79

The prosecution was led by Regional Prosecutor Merlynn Barola‑Uy, who stated after the sentencing:

"I hope this sends a very strong message to all abusers, all human traffickers, that crime really does not pay." ↗80

5.3 — The Sentence Means Reclusión Perpetua

Philippine courts use the term Reclusión perpetua, which is essentially life in prison without possibility of parole. ↗81 As a Canadian column described: "No faint clauses. No parole eligibility. It means your life is over." ↗82

PART VI

🕰️ Current Status (2022–2026)

6.1 — Incarceration and Concurrent Sentences

As of the latest available information (2025), Margallo is serving her 126‑year sentence in the Philippines. She will spend the rest of her natural life in prison. ↗83

Scully, already serving a life sentence from 2018, had his sentence extended to 129 years in 2022. Both remain in detention facilities in the Cagayan de Oro region (likely the Lumbia city jail where Margallo was originally remanded). ↗84

6.2 — Outstanding Cases and Wider Network

Reports indicate that even after the 2022 conviction, Margallo and Scully still faced at least 50 other qualified trafficking cases across multiple jurisdictions in Mindanao (Cagayan de Oro, Bukidnon, and Surigao). ↗85 Other members of the syndicate — Christian Johann Rothe, Alexander Lao, Haniel Caetano de Oliveira, and Marshall Ruskin — remained at large as of 2017. ↗86 However, the Brazilian doctor Dr. Haniel Caetano de Oliveira was arrested in Uberaba, Brazil, on December 21, 2015, and was later charged by the Brazilian Federal Public Ministry for integrating the NFL ring, sending money, and providing medical advice on how to drug victims. ↗87
